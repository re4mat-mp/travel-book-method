# Style of the book

Every session that writes or edits a chapter reads this file first. It exists because sessions forget rules
between conversations; a rule that lives only in a chat is gone the next morning. When you correct a chapter
twice for the same thing, the correction belongs here.

## Each day

- One chapter per day, about five to ten pages on a Kindle.
- Order inside the chapter: the place first (what it is, what happened there), then how people live with it,
  then festivals or beliefs if there are any, with one fact to tell at the table where it fits; then what to look
  at, then the sources.
- Five phrases of the day near the top, the kind you can actually use that day.
- Personal tastes (record shops, a favourite café) go at the end of the book, not into the days. The days are
  for everyone who reads the book.

## Tone

- Full sentences, the voice of a guide or a book. No lists of labels where a sentence would do.
- It should read well out loud.
- Titles that sound like chapters ("A city that kept its gold"), never like form fields ("What to do here").

## Content rules

- **No times, prices or bookings, anywhere.** Not even "it opens at seven". They change, and they live in your
  tickets and calendar. The book holds what doesn't change.
- **Every foreign word three ways:** script · romanisation · meaning. Example: 暖簾 · noren · the curtain over
  the door.
- **No fact without a source.** If a source exists but the fact isn't exact, keep it with careful wording
  ("several guides say…") rather than dropping it. No "(to be confirmed)" in the text.
- Sources go at the end of the chapter. When a source is commercial or a tourism board, say so.
- Don't advise against the itinerary. What's fixed is fixed.

## One number, one place

Counts drift. If the book says "three days" on the cover, in the index and in the introduction, those are three
places to forget to update. Keep a count in one place where you can, and when you change it, grep for it in the
text and in the plate code: a cover's subtitle is drawn into an image, and fixing the text never reaches it.
