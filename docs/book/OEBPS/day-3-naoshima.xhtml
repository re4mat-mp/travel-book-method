Day 3 · Naoshima

An island that turned into a museum

瀬戸内海 · Seto Naikai · the Seto Inland Sea

A small island in a calm sea between three of Japan's main islands. Fishing and a copper refinery, which still works at the north end; and, since the end of the twentieth century, art.

A calm sea full of islands

The Seto Inland Sea is a long stretch of water with hundreds of islands in it. For centuries it was Japan's main road: rice, salt and soldiers moved by boat between the west and the old capitals. The water is calm because the land almost closes around it.

By the late twentieth century many of the smaller islands were emptying out, as young people left for the cities. On a few of them, art became a way of bringing people back.

The pumpkin on the pier

The picture everyone knows from Naoshima is a yellow pumpkin covered in black dots, sitting at the end of a concrete pier. It is by Yayoi Kusama, an artist who has painted dots and pumpkins for most of her life. It has stood there since the 1990s.

In the summer of 2021 a typhoon picked it up and carried it into the sea. It broke into pieces, was taken away and remade, and went back on its pier the following year. So the pumpkin you see is, in a way, its second life.

One to tell at the table: the island has two of her pumpkins. The yellow one sits by the water; a red one waits at the ferry port, and you can walk inside it.

A museum under the ground

地中美術館 · Chichū bijutsukan · the museum inside the earth was designed by the architect Tadao Ando and is built almost entirely below the surface of a hill, so it does not spoil the view of the sea. Inside, there is very little artificial light. Paintings by Claude Monet from his water-lily series hang in a white room lit from above by daylight, so they look different in the morning, at noon and when it clouds over.

Visitors are asked to take off their shoes in that room. The floor is made of small white stones, and the light comes off it too.

What to look at today

The ferries, the colour of the sea, and the same painting twice, an hour apart: what changed is only the sky.
